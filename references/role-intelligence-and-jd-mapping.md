# Role Intelligence and JD Mapping

Read this reference whenever the user supplies a JD or asks for role analysis or resume tailoring.

## Sequence

1. Preserve the JD and split materially different responsibilities or qualifications into atomic lines.
2. Reopen the user's latest evidence bank, candidate profile, corrections, base resume, and relevant reusable content.
3. Screen hard eligibility requirements and separate blockers from capability fit.
4. Decode the role's business objective, users, operating loop, deliverables, stakeholders, and likely proof standard.
5. Research the company, industry, team, role, core products, user groups, and business model at the selected depth.
6. Form evidence-backed candidate views of the industry and role, then draft motivation and career direction for confirmation.
7. Map every material JD line to the user's exact evidence and identify gaps.
8. Ask focused questions before strengthening unsupported claims.
9. Produce one combined positioning and section-by-section resume strategy.
10. Draft and export only in the format and review sequence the user selected.

## Research depth and sources

For Quick analysis, external research is optional. For Standard and Deep analysis, use current sources when available:

- official company, product, recruiting, investor, and executive material;
- first-hand practitioner interviews, talks, profiles, or detailed posts;
- authoritative industry, regulatory, and trade sources;
- relevant job boards and professional communities;
- for Deep analysis, recent firsthand interview reports for the same or closest comparable role.

Choose channels based on the target market rather than hardcoding one platform. For mainland-China recruiting, Xiaohongshu, 牛客、知乎、脉脉 and official recruiting channels may be useful. For other markets, use locally relevant professional networks, employer-review sites, candidate communities, alumni networks, or practitioner sources when publicly accessible.

Search with the JD, company, role, product, competitor, and public business context. Do not expose the candidate's name, contact details, resume, evidence-bank text, or confidential project material to external services unless the user explicitly authorizes a specific disclosure.

Prefer current primary sources for leadership, product strategy, organization, and scale. Treat anonymous accounts and single-person reports as directional signals. Cite material claims near the claim and label them as:

- **Verified fact**
- **Practitioner pattern**
- **Interview-report pattern**
- **Inference**
- **Unknown**

Never invent team size, reporting lines, KPIs, leadership, revenue, or promotion paths.

## Hard eligibility gate

Before assigning a fit score, check explicit requirements such as education, major, graduation or recruiting cohort, experience length, location, availability, work authorization, language, travel or shift expectations, required portfolio, certifications, and non-negotiable technical skills.

Classify each material constraint as:

- **Eligible**
- **Unclear — ask**
- **Potential blocker**
- **Not a blocker but needs explanation**

Keep eligibility separate from capability fit. A candidate may have strong capability overlap and still face a formal blocker.

## Candidate-facing fit analysis

Use direct language in the user's requested output language. Cover:

- whether the role appears worth applying for;
- an approximate fit assessment when useful, with confidence and evidence rather than false precision;
- direct continuation, adjacent transition, or high-stretch transition;
- the strongest matching evidence and the largest gaps;
- what the role actually does and what adjacent role it should not be confused with.

When useful, translate the JD into a table with these semantic columns: JD statement, actual work, common deliverables, likely KPI, and core capability. Localize the headings.

## Why the candidate fits

Rank experiences by relevance, not resume order. For each important experience:

- explain the underlying correspondence to the target role;
- cite the exact verified work, metric, artifact, or decision;
- label the match `Direct`, `Adjacent`, `Weak`, or `Absent`;
- identify primary evidence, supporting evidence, and likely distractions;
- state wording boundaries and likely interview follow-ups.

Use only the current user's bank. Examples and prior resumes may demonstrate a reasoning style but cannot overwrite confirmed facts.

## Role-intelligence pack

At Standard or Deep depth, cover only what can be supported:

### Role

- mission, daily and weekly work, deliverables, KPIs, stakeholders, ownership boundaries, growth path, and product or business value;
- contradictions between the written JD, official messaging, and practitioner accounts.

### Team

- how the team creates revenue or strategic value;
- competitive advantage, priority directions, cross-functional dependencies, and known leadership;
- likely structure only when clearly labeled as inference, with questions the candidate can use to verify it.

### Company

- company development stage, core products and product portfolio, target users and economic buyers, user scenarios and pain points, commercial model, competitors, advantages, constraints, and current strategic signals relevant to the role;
- which core product or product line the role most likely supports and how that product connects to the rest of the company.

### Core products and user profiles

- product purpose, main use cases, user journey, value proposition, adoption or monetization logic, and important ecosystem participants;
- primary users, secondary users, buyers or decision makers, creators or suppliers, and internal users when relevant;
- user goals, pain points, triggers, barriers, retention or repeat-use drivers, and how the target role changes their experience;
- clearly distinguish sourced user evidence, practitioner pattern, and inference.

### Industry

- development stage, value chain, commercial models, growth drivers, bottlenecks, regulation or technology shifts, and implications for the role;
- two or three defensible candidate insights, clearly separated from sourced facts.

## Candidate understanding, motivation, and planning

Read [candidate profile and planning](candidate-profile-and-planning.md). Produce:

1. the candidate's evidence-backed understanding of the industry's stage, business model, value chain, changes, constraints, and implications for the role;
2. the candidate's understanding of the role's user, business problem, operating loop, outputs, KPIs, stakeholders, and product or business value;
3. a proposed job-search motivation connecting the candidate's prior evidence to the company, core product, team, and role;
4. a realistic near-term and medium-term career plan plus gaps to close.

Separate verified external facts, assistant inference, the user's stated view, and a proposed narrative awaiting user confirmation. Do not invent motivation or a career goal.

## Line-by-line JD map

Create one row per atomic JD line using localized headings for these fields:

| ID | JD statement | Actual work | Core capability | Deliverable / KPI | Candidate evidence | Match | Open question | Resume action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

For candidate evidence, name the exact experience, project, metric, artifact, and evidence-bank record. Resume actions should be concrete: keep, move up, rewrite, quantify, clarify, add after confirmation, or omit.

After the table, consolidate repeated lines into the most important hiring capabilities and identify the strongest evidence, weakest requirement, and highest-impact resume change.

## Interview-report internalization

At Deep depth or when explicitly requested, read [interview-experience-internalization.md](interview-experience-internalization.md). Integrate the findings into the role analysis rather than copying other candidates' answers.

## Positioning and resume actions

Use one combined section that covers:

- the impression the current resume creates;
- the target impression for this JD;
- a one-sentence candidate positioning statement;
- distracting positioning to avoid;
- the three facts a recruiter should remember after a quick scan;
- recommended section order;
- a section-level table containing module or experience, role value, action, emphasis, compression, and risk;
- example rewrites only when the evidence is sufficiently verified.

Do not delete or materially merge user-supplied content without approval.

## Focused questions

Ask when an answer could change match strength, ownership, wording risk, selection, or interview defensibility. Prioritize scope, personal versus team contribution, decision logic, artifacts, metric definition, dates, relationship type, and missing high-priority examples.

## Recommended output order

For Standard or Deep personalized analysis, use this order unless the user requests another:

1. source-material and candidate-profile readiness summary;
2. hard eligibility gate;
3. fit judgment and plain-language role explanation;
4. company and business model;
5. core products and user profiles;
6. industry and competitive context;
7. team and organizational context;
8. role mission, daily work, stakeholders, outputs, and KPIs;
9. candidate industry understanding and role understanding;
10. proposed motivation and career plan for confirmation;
11. comparable interview-report intelligence at Deep depth;
12. line-by-line JD map and consolidated capability matrix;
13. evidence gaps and focused questions;
14. combined positioning and section-level resume actions;
15. requested resume artifact after material questions are resolved.
